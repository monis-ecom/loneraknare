## The three buyer groups (desire differs by group)

| Group | Who | Core desire |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The user (self-buyer)** | Elderly / arthritis / stroke / one-handed adults buying for themselves | Keep doing it **myself** — dignity & independence |
| **B. The gift-buyer** | Adult children / spouse buying *for* the user | Give a parent independence back + **worry less** |
| **C. The clinical influencer** | Arbetsterapeuter (OTs), rehab, senior-care staff who recommend | A simple aid that **enables kvarboende** and is safe |

---

## Desire 1 — "I want to prepare food myself, without asking for help"

**The strongest driver.** Losing the ability to cut/peel/butter is one of the most visible, daily losses of autonomy.

- **Real signal [SEARCH-PARAPHRASE — Äldre i centrum]:** *"För vissa blev måltiden en symbol för självständighet, att klara sig själv."*
- **Real signal [SEARCH-PARAPHRASE — Kunskapsguiden]:** support that lets a person *"så långt som möjligt hantera mat och måltider själv"* is a key factor for wellbeing in older people.
- **Mechanism the product answers:** the spikes become the "second hand," so the task is possible solo.

**Frustration behind it [SEARCH-PARAPHRASE — Reumatikerförbundet / Artrosportalen]:** *"Aktiviteter som belastar handled och fingrar över tid, som att skala och skära"* are exactly what artros hands struggle with — i.e. the desire is blocked precisely at cutting and peeling.

## Desire 2 — "I don't want to be a burden"

The emotional undertow beneath the practical need. Accepting help carries shame; a tool that removes the need to ask **protects the relationship**.

- **Real signal [SEARCH-PARAPHRASE — Vårdfokus / Kunskapsguiden]:** among older people, *"stolthet, rädsla för att förlora sin självständighet eller oro för att bli en börda"* shape whether they'll accept help at all.
- **Real signal [SEARCH-PARAPHRASE — Kunskapsguiden]:** *"Att förlora sin självständighet är förknippat med stor sorg och kan väcka skam."*

**Copy implication:** sell *autonomy regained*, not *disability managed*. A tool the user operates alone sidesteps the shame of being helped.

## Desire 3 — "I want to stay safe and keep living in my own home"

Cutting with an unstable board and a knife in one hand is genuinely dangerous; a slip can cause a cut or a fall reaching to steady things.

- **Real signal [SEARCH-PARAPHRASE — Seniorlife]:** the value proposition is explicitly framed around a board with *"gummifötter på undersidan som gör att den inte glider iväg på bordet"* and edges that *"håller matvaran på plats"* — i.e. the market already sells this on **safety/stability**.
- **Real signal [SEARCH-PARAPHRASE — arbetsterapi research, DiVA/Umeå]:** aids *"möjliggör kvarboende och en självständig vardag"*; fall-prevention work notes fall injuries *"kan minskas med upp till 30 procent."*

**Copy implication (Group B & C):** for the gift-buyer and the OT, "safe, stable, won't slide" is the trust anchor.

> Verbatim quote: `[behöver verifieras]`

## Desire 4 (gift-buyer) — "I want to help my parent and worry less"

- **Real signal [SEARCH-PARAPHRASE — presentguiden / DealGuru senior-gift guides]:** practical, everyday-easing tools ("smarta prylar som förenklar i köket", a shopping trolley that gives *"friheten att själva kunna transportera hem matvaror"*) are a recognised category of gifts to elderly parents — meaning there is an existing **gift-buying motion** for "restore-independence" products.
- **Emotion:** guilt + love + distance. The child can't be there for every meal; the tool is a proxy for care.

**Copy implication:** a clean gift narrative — *"Ge mamma/pappa självständigheten tillbaka."*

> Verbatim quote: `[behöver verifieras]`

## Desire 5 — "I want to feel like myself again" (post-diagnosis re-enablement)

For stroke survivors and newly-arthritic users, a lost skill regained is emotionally outsized — proof the condition hasn't taken everything.

- **Real signal [SEARCH-PARAPHRASE — Strokeförbundet]:** for a *"förlamad arm"*, a *"fixplatta"* where *"stålpiggarna kan hålla fast tex grönsaker som du vill skära"* is presented as an enabling aid — the product is literally positioned as *"laga mat med en hand."*
- **Real signal [Socialstyrelsen, VERIFIED-level stat]:** ~3 of 4 stroke cases occur in people over 70; only ~4% (≈1,000/yr) are under 50 — so the stroke sub-avatar skews older, overlapping heavily with the elderly/independence avatar.

## Competitor / market angle read (how the desire is already sold)

From what is publicly visible on the Swedish market (Seniorlife, Varsam "Fixbrädan", Iris Hjälpmedel, Reuma Shop, keepon.se):
- The category is sold **functionally and clinically** ("piggar", "halkfri", "för dig med en hand", "hjälpmedel"), rarely **emotionally**.
- **Gap / opportunity:** almost nobody sells the **dignity/independence story** or the **gift-from-child angle** — they sell a spec. On a Jantelagen-sensitive market that distrusts hype, a *quietly emotional, proof-led* positioning ("do it yourself again — safely") is open territory.
- **Sophistication read:** the *mechanism* (spikes + non-slip) is **Stage 3–4** — customers have seen pin-boards before, so the win is **improved mechanism + new information + new identity/dignity framing**, not claiming to have invented food-fixation.

## Summary — desire hierarchy (for Phase E)

1. **Do it myself (independence/dignity)** ← primary
2. **Don't be a burden** (relational shame) ← emotional engine
3. **Stay safe / keep living at home** (safety + kvarboende) ← trust anchor
4. **Help my parent, worry less** (gift-buyer)
5. **Feel like myself again** (re-enablement)
